## Why does selling the company shake my sense of self?

Because you spent years inside it. The company gave you a role, a daily purpose and an answer to the question "what do you do?". When the company is no longer yours, those answers go quiet at once.

It can help to know this is common. It does not mean you made the wrong call. It means something important to you has changed hands.

## How do I introduce myself now?

At first, awkwardly. Many founders reach for the old line out of habit. Others skip the question altogether and avoid social events.

Try describing what you care about instead of what you were. You do not need a polished line. A true, unfinished one is fine: "I'm taking some time, and thinking about what's next."

## Where does purpose come from if not from the business?

From smaller, closer things. People you want to spend time with, problems you find yourself drawn to, ways you like to be useful. These were always there. They were just crowded out by the company.

### A simple exercise

Write down the last three moments in the past month when you felt properly like yourself. Notice what they have in common. It is rarely a job title.

## Where to go from here

If it still feels empty, give it time and give it company. Talking it through with someone who understands founders can make the gap feel less lonely. This is not therapy or medical advice. It is practical support for a big change.
