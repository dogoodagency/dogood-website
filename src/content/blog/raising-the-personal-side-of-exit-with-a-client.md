## How do I bring it up without overstepping?

Keep it light and curious. You are not diagnosing anything. You are simply showing that you see the whole person, not only the spreadsheet. Try phrases like:

- "Beyond the deal itself, what are you looking forward to?"
- "What do you picture the week after closing?"
- "Who else is in this with you?"

Then listen. If the answer is vague, you have learned something, and you have not said anything awkward.

## What are the signs a founder needs this conversation?

Look at behaviour, not words. Common signals include:

- Slow responses on decisions that have been settled for weeks.
- Reopening terms that were already agreed.
- Talk of "one more year" when the plan was clear.
- Little to say about what comes after.

None of these prove anything. They are good reasons to ask one more question.

### Timing matters

Raise it early in the process, when it feels like normal planning. At the eleventh hour, it can feel like a problem.

## What do I do when the client says they are fine?

Take them at their word and leave the door open. You might say, "Good. If that changes, I know someone who works with founders on the personal side." Plant the idea, then let it rest.
