## Why are we reacting so differently to the same sale?

You shared the company, but you did not share the same role in it. One of you may have been out front, the other behind the scenes. You may also grieve, celebrate and rest in different ways. That is normal, and it is worth saying out loud.

## How do we start a conversation without it turning into a decision?

Make it small and give it a frame. A walk, a coffee, twenty minutes after dinner. Try opening with something like:

- "What was the best part of today?"
- "What do you miss already?"
- "What are you glad is over?"

Agree up front that this is a talk, not a meeting. Nobody has to leave with an answer.

### Keep the kids and the team in the loop

If you have children or close colleagues who are also affected, a short, honest update goes a long way. They often sense more than we think.

## What if we want different things next?

Then you have found something useful. Different wishes are easier to work with once they are visible. Write each wish on its own card and look at them together. You may find more overlap than you expected, and what does not overlap can often be sequenced, not fought over.

None of this replaces professional advice on money, law or relationships. If a conversation keeps getting stuck, a neutral third person can help.
