## Why would a founder stall a deal they asked for?

Because wanting to sell and being ready to leave are not the same thing. The company has been their work, their identity and often their social world. The offer is real, but so is the loss, and sometimes the loss only becomes clear when the pen is in hand.

## How can I tell readiness from ordinary negotiation?

Negotiation has reasons you can point to: price, terms, risk. Readiness issues tend to be vaguer. Watch for:

- Concerns that shift each time you address one.
- New conditions that do not connect to the numbers.
- Delays around signing, not around diligence.
- Anxiety about the announcement or the handover.

If the objections keep changing shape, the real question may be personal.

### What it costs

A stalled deal wears on everyone: the buyer's patience, the team's morale, the founder's own confidence. A founder who is quietly unready is rarely in a good position to negotiate either.

## What can I do without stepping outside my role?

You do not need to coach. A few practical moves:

- Ask early what they plan to do after closing.
- Normalise the question: "Most founders find this part harder than expected."
- Offer a trusted place to talk it through, separate from the deal team.

That is all. You remain the expert on the plan, and someone else supports the person.
